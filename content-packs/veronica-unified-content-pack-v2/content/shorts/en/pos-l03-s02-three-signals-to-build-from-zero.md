Posting every day can still leave you invisible. If you are starting from zero, build these three signals before you chase followers.

First: a clear offer. People should understand the problem you solve and who it is for.

Second: useful content. Let people see how you think before you ask them to buy.

Third: relevant context. Show up where the people and conversations in your field already exist.

For example, if you help independent hotels improve direct bookings, make the offer name that problem, publish a booking-page teardown, and join one place where hotel operators already discuss acquisition. Each signal makes the others easier to believe.

Then add proof as it appears: a pilot, a small result, a useful collaboration, a testimonial, a case study, or even a thoughtful analysis of a real problem. The objective is not to pretend you have ten years of evidence when you do not. It is to make the evidence you do have coherent.

This matters because early-stage positioning is fragile. If every week you change the offer, audience, topic, and message, the market has to relearn who you are from zero.

Consistency gives each new signal something useful to build on.

At the beginning, alignment beats volume. Ten coherent signals around one expertise can build more authority than fifty unrelated pieces of content. Do not try to look established everywhere. Make one useful association become stronger every week.
