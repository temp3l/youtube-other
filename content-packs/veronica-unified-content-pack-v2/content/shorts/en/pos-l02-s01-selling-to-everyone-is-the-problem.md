Broad messaging feels safe until the right customer scrolls past it. Imagine a consultant whose homepage says, “I help businesses grow.” A freelancer, restaurant owner, SaaS founder, and hotel manager can all read it — and none knows whether it is really for them. That is the hidden cost of “for everyone.”

A niche solves that problem. It does not mean nobody outside the niche can buy from you. It means one group can immediately recognize, “This is for me.” Specificity is not a restriction. It is a reduction in interpretation work.

Compare “I help businesses grow” with “I help independent service professionals turn expertise into an offer clients understand quickly.” The second statement excludes some people, but it also helps the right buyer recognize themselves, the problem, and the promised direction immediately.

There is another benefit: specific positioning improves everything downstream. Your examples become more relevant. Your content gets easier to plan. Referrals become easier because people know what sentence to use when recommending you. Even sales calls improve because the prospect arrives with a clearer expectation.

The mistake is assuming a broad message creates a broad market. Often it creates weak relevance across a broad market. A narrower message can create stronger demand from the people most likely to buy.

More possible buyers do not automatically create more confident buyers.

Before making your offer broader, ask whether making it clearer would work better.
