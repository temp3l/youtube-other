Here is a fast way to make an “expert” bio look less credible: write the word expert, then give people no evidence for it. A profile says “conversion expert,” but shows generic posts, no teardown, no case, and ten unrelated services. The title asks for trust before the profile has earned it.

Positioning is the accumulation of these signals. You do not need to look huge or famous. You need enough coherent evidence for people to reach the conclusion themselves.

If you want to be known for conversion strategy, do not rely on the word “expert.” Publish a teardown that shows what you notice. Explain a decision you made. Show an outcome. Let clients describe the result in their own words. Answer questions that only someone close to the problem would understand.

None of those signals is dramatic alone. Together they reduce uncertainty. And that is what authority really does: it makes the buyer feel they are taking less risk.

A useful rule is this: claims are weak when they require trust first. Evidence is strong because it helps create trust. So instead of asking, “What title should I put in my bio?” ask, “What would someone need to see repeatedly to believe this title without me saying it?”

The strongest form of expert positioning is when other people start using the label before you do.
