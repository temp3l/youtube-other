You are probably more interested in your solution than your customer is.

You know the framework, the process, the software, the methodology, the features. You have spent months or years thinking about how it works.

The customer usually starts somewhere else.

They start with a problem they recognize or a result they want.

They are not searching for your five-step method because they do not know your five-step method exists. They are thinking, “I keep losing clients,” “Nobody understands what I do,” “My team is wasting time,” or “I need this to become easier.”

That means good positioning starts before the solution.

First, show that you understand the situation they are already experiencing. Name the problem in language they would use. Make the cost of leaving it unresolved visible. Or make the desired future specific enough that they can picture it.

Only then does your solution become interesting.

This is why technically accurate marketing can still fail. It explains the answer before the buyer feels the question.

Your customer does not need to fall in love with your process first. They need to recognize themselves in the problem and believe the outcome matters.

Start there. Then show them why your solution is the bridge.

A simple test: read your headline and first paragraph. If they mostly describe your framework, technology, or credentials, rewrite them around the situation that makes somebody search for help in the first place.
