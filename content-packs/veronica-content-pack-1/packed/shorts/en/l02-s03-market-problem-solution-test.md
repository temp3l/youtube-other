If your positioning feels vague, answer three questions. One: which market do you understand well enough to describe specifically? Two: what problem does that group already recognize in its own words? Three: how does your solution connect directly to that problem? If one answer is fuzzy, the whole position gets fuzzy. Most businesses start with the thing they want to sell. Here is where businesses often reverse the sequence. They start with a package, a method, or a feature they want to sell and then search for people who might want it. Positioning works better in the other direction. Understand a real group, describe a problem they already feel, then show why your solution is a logical response. The buyer should recognize the problem before you ask them to value the solution. Customers start with what they are experiencing. Strong positioning connects the two through a precise understanding of the market.
