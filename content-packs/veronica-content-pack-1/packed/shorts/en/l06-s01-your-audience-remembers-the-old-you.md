Your business can change faster than your audience’s memory. You may have moved into a new field, built new skills, and created a new offer, while people still associate you with what you did two years ago. That does not mean the new direction is wrong. It means the market’s mental model has not caught up yet. Repositioning starts by identifying the association that already exists, then deliberately building a new one. This is why a sudden new offer can confuse loyal followers more than new ones. New people only see the current version. Existing followers compare it with years of old signals. If you were known for one subject and now sell something different, they need a bridge: what changed, what carried over, and why the new expertise makes sense. Without that bridge, a logical evolution inside your business can look random from outside. You are not just changing a service. You are changing what problem should make people think of your name.
