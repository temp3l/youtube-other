Try this positioning test. Ask yourself what you want to be known for. Then ask a client or colleague to describe what they think you are known for, without showing them your answer. If the two answers match, your positioning is communicating clearly. If they do not, that gap is valuable information. It tells you what the market currently sees. Positioning is not what you intended to communicate. Make the test more useful by asking three people separately and writing down their exact words. Do not ask whether they like your brand. Ask what they would recommend you for. If one says strategy, another says social media, and a third cannot answer, you have learned more than a brand survey would tell you. You are measuring the category your name actually occupies in memory. It is what people actually remember. And when the right problem appears, memory matters because the person they remember is often the person they contact.
