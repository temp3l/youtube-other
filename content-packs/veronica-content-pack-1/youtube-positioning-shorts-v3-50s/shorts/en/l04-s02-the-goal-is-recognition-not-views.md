A viral post can give you attention without giving you positioning. The better question is not only, “How many people saw this?” Ask, “What did they learn to associate with my name?” Positioning content repeatedly connects three things: a topic, your name, and useful knowledge. When that happens often enough, people can retrieve you from memory when the relevant problem appears. Views measure exposure. Recognition measures whether that exposure built something useful. This changes what you measure. A post with fifty thousand views from random people may do less for your business than a post with three thousand views that generates two relevant introductions and several messages saying, ‘I thought of you when this happened.’ Those responses show retrieval, not just exposure. Attention disappears quickly. A useful association can keep working long after the post leaves the feed. You do not need every post to go viral. You need enough people to remember the right thing about you.
