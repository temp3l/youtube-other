The fastest way to weaken a positioning book is to put everything you know into it. Marketing, productivity, motivation, branding, mindset, sales, communication — suddenly the reader has no idea what expertise the book is supposed to represent. A stronger book usually does the opposite. Choose one problem, one reader, and one professional territory you want to become associated with. Use a one-sentence test before you write: ‘This book helps this kind of reader solve or understand this specific problem.’ If that sentence needs five commas, the position is probably too broad. Depth gives you plenty of material without changing the promise. You can use stories, mistakes, methods, objections, and examples while still teaching the market one clear association between your name and the subject. You can explore that subject deeply through stories, examples, mistakes, and methods without losing the position. A book becomes useful for positioning when somebody can easily explain why they should recommend it — and why your name belongs with that topic.
