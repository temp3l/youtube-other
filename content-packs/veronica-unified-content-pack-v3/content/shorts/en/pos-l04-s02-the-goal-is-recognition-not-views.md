A viral post can give you attention without giving you positioning. The better question is not, “How many people saw this?” Ask, “What did they learn to associate with my name?”

Positioning content repeatedly connects three things: a topic, your name, and useful knowledge. With repetition, people retrieve you when the relevant problem appears.

Views measure exposure. Recognition measures whether that exposure built something useful.

This changes measurement. A post with fifty thousand views from random people may do less for your business than a post with three thousand views that generates two relevant introductions and several messages saying, “I thought of you when this happened.” Those responses show retrieval, not just exposure.

You can audit this. Look at your last ten pieces of content and ask: if someone saw only these, what would they think I know? Then look at your inbound messages, referrals, profile visits, and sales conversations. Are people using the language you want associated with you? Are they bringing you the problems you want to solve?

Attention disappears quickly. A useful association can keep working long after the post leaves the feed.

You do not need every post to go viral. You need enough of the right people to repeatedly connect your name with the right problem. Recognition is what turns content from exposure into positioning.
