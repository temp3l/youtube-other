Open your website and pretend you have never seen your business before. Give yourself five seconds. Can you tell who it is for, what problem it solves, and why the visitor should keep reading? If not, adding more sections may not help.

Your website does not have to explain your entire career on the first screen. It needs to give the visitor the right category fast enough that the rest of the page makes sense.

Try a brutal test. Show only the first screen to someone who does not know you. Hide it after five seconds. Then ask: Who is this for? What do they help with? Why would I continue? If the answers are vague, the page is making the visitor do interpretation work. Most visitors will not do that work. They will leave.

Another mistake is trying to sound sophisticated instead of specific. Polished language can hide the actual problem you solve.

Design can amplify a clear position, but it cannot rescue a missing one. A simple one-page site with a precise promise can position you better than a beautiful site full of broad language.

Clarity first. Then use the rest of the page to prove the promise with examples, evidence, objections, and a clear next step. Your homepage should not make people decode you. It should help the right person recognize themselves.
