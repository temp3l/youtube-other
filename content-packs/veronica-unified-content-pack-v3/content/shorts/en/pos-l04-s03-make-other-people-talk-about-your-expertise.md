One of the strongest positioning signals happens when you are not in the room. Someone asks, “Do you know anybody who can help with this?” and another person says your name.

Content can help create that moment. Explain your field clearly, interview people from adjacent areas, participate in relevant conversations, and make your knowledge easy to share. The goal is not only to tell people what you do. It is to give them language they can repeat.

“She does marketing” is weak. “She helps boutique hotels turn direct-booking data into better offers” is much easier to pass on. The second sentence contains a customer, a problem area, and a result. That is why it travels better through referrals.

Now ask yourself: could someone recommend you accurately after seeing three of your posts? If they needed to open your profile first to remember what you actually do, the position is still too dependent on explanation.

Your content should repeatedly supply portable sentences through examples, frameworks, stories, and specific points of view. Over time, the audience begins using those sentences for you.

When people can describe your relevance accurately without checking your profile, you have created a portable position.

The goal is not simply to be visible. It is to make the right association easy for other people to carry.
