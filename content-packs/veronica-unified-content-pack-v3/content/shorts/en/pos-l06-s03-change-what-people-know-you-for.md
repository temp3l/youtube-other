If you are repositioning your business, run this ninety-day audit before you publish anything.

Label every public signal as OLD, NEW, or NEITHER.

OLD means it reinforces what people already know you for. NEW means it strengthens the position you want next. NEITHER means it uses attention without helping either direction.

Now look at the balance.

If your bio announces a new specialty but eight of your next ten posts still reinforce the old one, the market is receiving a mixed instruction. If your website changes but your case studies, collaborations, examples, and conversations stay anchored in the past, the old association keeps winning through repetition.

You do not need to erase your history. Some old signals may still generate revenue or provide useful proof. The objective is controlled transition.

Increase NEW evidence across ninety days with examples, offers, collaborations, and explanations that connect old expertise to the new direction.

Then audit the market’s language.

Why are people contacting you? How do they introduce you? What topic do they tag you in? Which opportunities arrive without you explaining the change first?

When other people begin using the new category spontaneously, you have evidence that the repositioning is moving from your communication into their memory.

Do not measure a repositioning campaign by how much you posted. Measure whether the market’s description of you actually changed.
