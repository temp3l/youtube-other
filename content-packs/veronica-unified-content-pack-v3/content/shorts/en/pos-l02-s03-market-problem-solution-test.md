If your positioning feels vague, run this three-part test: market, problem, solution.

Imagine a consultant says, “I do marketing for businesses.” Market: vague. Problem: missing. Solution: vague. Nothing sticks.

Now make it concrete: “I help independent consultants turn confusing expertise into an offer clients understand and buy.”

The market is independent consultants. The problem is expertise that is hard to explain. The solution is a clearer, buyable offer.

That is why the order matters.

Most businesses start with the thing they want to sell—a package, method, or feature—and then search for people who might want it. Strong positioning moves the other way. Start with a real group. Listen to what they are already experiencing. Describe the problem in language they recognize. Then show why your solution is a logical response.

Now pressure-test your version.

Can you name five real people or companies that fit the market? Do they already recognize the problem without a twenty-minute explanation? Can you explain your solution without internal jargon? And does the outcome matter enough that someone would pay to change it?

If one answer is fuzzy, the whole position becomes fuzzy.

The buyer should recognize themselves before you ask them to value what you sell.

Market. Problem. Solution.

When those three click together quickly, your positioning becomes easier to understand, easier to remember, and easier to buy right now.
