You can change your profile description in thirty seconds. Repositioning takes longer because you are not editing text — you are updating memory.

Your offer, website, content, examples, conversations, and professional story all need to reinforce the new direction. Some people will understand immediately. Others will keep seeing the old version of you for a while. That overlap is normal.

Think of every touchpoint as a vote for either the old position or the new one. If the bio changes but the homepage, examples, collaborations, and next ten posts still reinforce the old category, the old category wins.

This is why repositioning often feels slower than expected. You are competing with accumulated evidence. Someone who followed you for three years may have hundreds of old signals in memory. A new tagline cannot outweigh all of them by itself.

Instead, define the new association clearly and repeat the evidence long enough for people to encounter the same story in different contexts. Then watch for changes in language: new types of inquiries, different introductions, different topics people tag you in.

People need repeated proof over time.

A new bio announces the position. Repeated evidence makes the position believable. Consistent repetition makes it memorable. Time helps, but time alone does not reposition you. The evidence has to change too.
