Delete the words “expert,” “specialist,” and “strategist” from your profile. Would the rest of the page still prove what you are good at?

That is harder than writing a stronger bio.

A buyer should not have to trust the label. They should be able to infer the expertise from the evidence around it. Look at your offer, case studies, examples, testimonials, and content. Do they make the same conclusion easier to reach?

If you say you are a conversion specialist, but your case studies discuss general marketing, your content jumps between productivity and branding, and your testimonials only say you were “great to work with,” the label is doing almost all the work.

Now imagine removing the label and leaving behind a clear offer, before-and-after examples, explanations of conversion mistakes, client results, and testimonials that describe the same problem. The position survives because the evidence carries it.

Run this audit on your own public presence. Remove the status words mentally. Ask: what expertise would a stranger infer from what remains?

If the answer is vague, do not add a bigger claim. Improve the evidence. Make the offer more specific. Show better examples. Ask testimonials to describe the problem and outcome.

Strong positioning does not require sounding impressive. It requires your proof, examples, and decisions to make the right conclusion difficult to miss.
