# Narration Script

The book had no author, but the main character had my name.

Nina Vale was a graduate student, the sort of practical person who checked locks, logs, and timestamps before trusting rumors. At first, a university archive after hours felt like a maintenance problem, not a haunting. Old structures complained. Bad wiring made patterns. Witnesses turned ordinary fear into warnings. Then Nina heard the rule that made every later detail harder to dismiss: Never read past the page where you fall asleep.

The first sign was an unlisted archive volume. It was not hidden. It sat in the open, clean and deliberate, with no broken lock, no muddy scramble around it, and no person on the nearest camera. Under the rain, dust, or silence of the place, Nina kept noticing the same three sounds: page turns, archive lights clicking off, and pencil writing by itself. At first they belonged to the building. Later, they sounded like instructions.

Nina handled it like an incident report. Photos were taken from fixed angles. Times were written down. Doors, windows, meters, locks, and storage spaces were checked twice. Each note had a practical label: draft, old hardware, bad sensor, tired eyes. The first ordinary explanation lasted only until the next check revealed new chapters describing Nina's day before she lived it. That was the first moment the fear became specific. It was not a scream in the dark. It was a documented contradiction, and every documented contradiction was harder to forget.

After that, the authorless book stopped behaving like a random disturbance. Each time Nina tested one detail, another detail answered. A lock returned closed after being photographed open. A reflection showed movement before the room moved. A sound that should have stayed behind Nina came from ahead. The object from the first sign never reappeared in the same position twice, yet every photo insisted it had always been there. The rule no longer sounded like superstition. It sounded like the only surviving instruction from someone who had already failed.

Leaving would have been the sensible choice: lock up, send the notes, call for help, and wait for daylight. Nina almost did. But the contradiction — new chapters describing Nina's day before she lived it — meant the danger was not just atmospheric. It selected people, repeated steps, and used proof as bait. If Nina walked away with only half the pattern understood, the next practical person would enter the same scene with less warning.

The oldest record made it worse: The archive catalog listed the book as returned by students who were later removed from enrollment records. That detail did not explain everything, but it connected too many points to ignore. Earlier witnesses had measured, argued, documented, and resisted. Their mistake was not stupidity. Their mistake was believing that once the pattern had been named, it could be controlled.

Then the danger became personal. The book revised Nina's memories by changing sentences about her childhood. The terror worked because it was exact. It knew which sound could freeze a hand above a switch, which memory could make caution feel cruel, and which urgent message could make obedience seem cowardly. Nina stopped trying to win. The goal became smaller: survive the next minute without giving the place a cleaner invitation.

The second confirmation came in pieces, which made it worse. One timestamp matched. One direction matched. One small sensory detail matched the warning exactly. Described out loud, it sounded theatrical. Seen in order, it was painfully physical: first an unlisted archive volume; then new chapters describing Nina's day before she lived it; then the book revised Nina's memories by changing sentences about her childhood. The impossible did not arrive as one grand reveal. It built itself from ordinary evidence until ordinary evidence could no longer hold it.

The first real plan failed because it was reasonable. She stopped reading, hid the volume in quarantine storage, and photographed every page. At first, the setup held. The checks were clean. The boundaries looked normal enough to trust. Then the breach appeared from the wrong side of the precaution, without disturbing the thing the precaution was meant to protect. That was when Nina understood the trap: the danger did not punish recklessness. It punished confidence.

The book remained locked while Nina's phone showed no new images. The silence afterward was almost convincing. It gave Nina enough room to feel foolish and to imagine a report made of plain words: stress, weather, fatigue, faulty equipment. Even the shadows settled into normal shapes for a while. Then the motif returned — page turns, archive lights clicking off, and pencil writing by itself — not louder, just closer. No one else would have called it a scream. By then, Nina understood that subtle sounds were how the place announced ownership.

The final attempt depended on obeying the rule exactly, not heroically. No bargaining. No challenge. No last look for better proof. Every movement had to stay small, boring, and disciplined. That restraint almost worked. Then the thing offered the one reason most likely to break it: a familiar voice, a visible rescue, a message urgent enough to override memory. Nina waited until the impulse passed. In that pause, the truth became visible.

The final verification removed the last ordinary explanation. Her thesis document opened with chapter thirty-one in the same typeface. A single camera could still be doubted. A timestamp could be blamed on hardware. A witness could be dismissed as tired. Together, the pieces formed proof, but not comfort. They agreed on the one fact Nina most wanted to reject: the rule had never been meant to save everyone. It had only been meant to decide who would be noticed last.

By morning, the official version was smaller than the truth. It mentioned weather, stress, equipment failure, and a misread warning. It left out the moment Nina understood that survival had not ended the pattern. It had made Nina part of the pattern. The place, the object, the sound, and the rule remained connected, waiting for the next careful person to test them.

The final page said, The reader becomes the author when there is no one left to blame.
