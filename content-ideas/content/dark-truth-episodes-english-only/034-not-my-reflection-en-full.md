# Narration Script

Elena Marks raised her hand toward the wardrobe mirror. Her reflection lowered its own hand and pointed behind her.

Nothing stood in the bedroom doorway. The apartment was still, the laptop on her desk was asleep, and the only sound was the refrigerator clicking off in the kitchen. Elena told herself the glass had caught a delayed movement from the hallway. Then the reflection lifted one finger to its lips before Elena moved at all.

Elena worked from home as a graphic designer, and the mirrored wardrobe doors filled the wall behind her desk. She could not simply leave the apartment that night because a client presentation was due by morning, her rent depended on the contract, and her mother was waiting for the money Elena had promised to send. She moved her desk lamp, closed the wardrobe doors, and returned to the video call.

On the call, her friend Mara stopped speaking mid-sentence. Mara asked why Elena had smiled. Elena had not. In the small preview window, the reflected Elena smiled again, one second early, as if demonstrating the delay for both of them. Elena restarted the camera, recorded the screen, and marked the time: 11:17 p.m.

Steam on the bathroom mirror gave the next warning. The words appeared backward from Elena's side of the glass: DO NOT LET HER FINISH YOUR MOVEMENT. Elena photographed the message before it faded. The letters were not scratched into the surface. They had formed inside the condensation, each stroke drawn by a finger that had pressed from the wrong side.

Elena tested the warning with ordinary movements. She raised a mug, touched her hair, and stepped backward. The reflection copied her until Elena hesitated. Then it finished the gesture for her. When Elena repeated that finished gesture, a memory slipped away. She looked at a framed beach photograph and could no longer remember who had taken it, though Mara's name was written on the back.

The rule became clear before midnight. The reflection could move first only after Elena watched it. If Elena copied what it began, the other Elena took a small piece of her life: a memory, a habit, a proof that tied Elena to the world outside the glass. If Elena refused to copy it, the reflection froze until she looked away.

Elena covered the wardrobe with sheets and taped paper over the bathroom mirror. For ten minutes the apartment felt normal. Then the black laptop screen showed a room that was not hers. The reflected apartment had the same desk and bed, but the posters were reversed, the windows opened onto a dark hallway, and another Elena stood inside it wearing the gray sweater Elena had lost years earlier.

The other Elena pressed both palms against the laptop glass. Her mouth moved without sound. Reversed whispering came through the speakers after the laptop battery had died. Mara called again, frightened, because the recording from the video call showed Elena sitting still while the reflected Elena smiled, wrote on the glass, and practiced Elena's signature.

Elena searched old tenant messages and maintenance reports from the building. Three former renters had complained about condensation writing, mirrored rooms on powered-off screens, and photographs that changed after midnight. One woman had moved out after all pictures of her sister went blank. Another had broken a bathroom mirror and then stopped appearing in security footage.

The thing behind the glass wanted repetition, not attention alone. It learned from every copied gesture and used the stolen pieces to become more convincing. It wore Elena's expressions, then her posture, then the tired half-smile she used when speaking to her mother. That was what made leaving hard. If Elena ran without proving which version was real, the reflection could answer Mara, call her mother, and step into Elena's life with better evidence than Elena had.

Elena needed a test the reflection could not predict. She had a thin scar under her left thumb from a childhood sewing needle. No photograph showed it clearly, and Elena never touched it on camera. She held her hand near the covered wardrobe and watched the sheet. A hand pressed out beneath the fabric, scarred under the right thumb.

The reflection had learned the scar but not its side.

Elena called Mara and told her to come upstairs only if Elena sent one sentence: Ask me which thumb. Then Elena turned every lamp toward the wardrobe, uncovered the mirror, and stood with a hammer in her left hand. The reflected Elena smiled first. It raised its right hand, palm open, inviting Elena to mirror the movement and surrender the last proof.

Elena almost did it when the reflection spoke in her mother's voice. It said the money could wait, that Elena sounded exhausted, that she should rest and let someone else finish. The words hurt because they used exactly what Elena wanted to hear. Elena lowered her hand, not to copy the reflection, but to touch the scar on the left thumb.

The reflected Elena touched the wrong thumb.

Elena swung the hammer before the other version could recover. The wardrobe mirror cracked from corner to corner. Glass hum filled the room, then cut into silence. The reflection did not scream. It pressed its face calmly to the breaking surface and smiled with Elena's mouth, as if the plan had cost Elena more than she understood.

Mara found Elena on the floor among the glass. The sheets had fallen, the laptop was dead, and the bathroom mirror was clear. Elena remembered Mara, her mother, and the beach photograph again. She thought the broken mirror had returned what had been taken.

Then Mara took a picture for the police report.

The photograph showed the shattered wardrobe, the gray sweater on the floor, and Mara kneeling beside empty space. Behind them, where the mirror had been, a second apartment stood in perfect focus. In that apartment, Elena's reflection faced the camera with the scar under the correct thumb.

Every photograph after that was the same. Elena could speak, breathe, sign her name, and touch the people who loved her. Cameras recorded the room around her, but not Elena herself. The final proof was not that the reflection had escaped. It was that the world now treated Elena like the thing that belonged behind glass.
